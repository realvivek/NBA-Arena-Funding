COMPARISON: public money after a documented relocation threat vs. deals without one (NBA home-city arena deals, 1993-2026)
Generated from threat_ledger.csv; dollars are nominal public CAPITAL commitments converted to 2025 dollars with approximate CPI-U annual averages.

CLASSIFICATION RULE
- Threat group (T): the owner or the league made an explicit threat (named another city, filed/announced a move, sold to a buyer intending to move, or the league stated it would move/buy the team) or an implied threat (talks with another city, "exploring options", invoking or holding a lease out clause, sale to an out-of-town buyer with no commitment to keep the team, sale offered with no relocation restriction) during the same arena campaign that produced the approval.
- No-threat group (N): no such owner/league statement found. Includes cases where only public officials cited relocation risk (OKC 2023, flagged) and two 1998-99 cases with unclear records (DAL 1998, SAS 1999, flagged).
- Excluded: operating-only deals (IND 2010 $33.5M; MEM 2022 $44.8M; both threat cases), the 1997 Sacramento loan, unexecuted term sheets (SAC 2012), pending deals (MEM 2024, POR 2026, HOU 2026), and destination-city money (reported separately below).
- Public share = public capital / total project cost where both are known (n/a for MIN 1994 purchase, CHA 2022, UTA 2024).

CAVEATS
- Small samples (9 vs 13); p-values are descriptive only; none of the differences is statistically distinguishable from chance. Deal sizes are driven by era, arena age and scope (new build vs renovation), so a raw comparison cannot isolate the effect of a threat.
- Selection: threats are documented mostly where reporters covered them; "no documented threat" is not proof none was made privately. Several no-threat deals were renovations required by lease clauses (e.g., CHA 2022 obligations, IND 2019).
- Capital only: operating subsidies (MIA $6.4M/yr; IND CIB ~$12.5M+/yr; MEM $44.8M) and tax exemptions are excluded; including them would raise totals for some threat cases.
- CPI: FRED CPIAUCNS annual averages (2025 uses 11 months; October 2025 was not published), the series the funding ledger uses.

EVIDENCE ON PAYOFF (task 4)
- Bradbury, J.C., Coates, D. & Humphreys, B.R. (2023). "The impact of professional sports franchises and venues on local economies: A comprehensive survey." Journal of Economic Surveys 37(4): 1389-1431. doi:10.1111/joes.12533 (online 2022-09-07). Abstract: >130 studies over 30+ years "continue to confirm the decades-old consensus of very limited economic impacts of professional sports teams and stadiums. Even with added nonpecuniary social benefits ... welfare improvements from hosting teams tend to fall well short of covering public outlays."
- Gayer, T., Drukker, A.J. & Gold, A.K. (2016). "Tax-exempt municipal bonds and the financing of professional sports stadiums." Brookings Economic Studies, Sept 2016: of 45 major-league venues built or significantly renovated since 2000, 36 used tax-exempt bonds ($13.0B principal); present-value federal subsidy $3.2B (about $3.7B including bondholder windfall). Updated: Drukker, Gayer & Gold (2020), National Tax Journal 73(1): 157-196, doi:10.17310/ntj.2020.1.05 - 57 venues, 43 with tax-exempt financing, $3.6B PV subsidy, $4.3B federal revenue loss.
- Promised vs delivered (NBA arenas):
  1) Barclays Center / Atlantic Yards (Nets): 2014 agreement required 2,250 affordable units by 2025-05-31; about 1,374 built and 876 not started; $2,000/month per missing unit penalties (>$1.75M/month) waived by Empire State Development after the developer threatened to sue (Gothamist, 2025). IBO (2009): arena a net loss to the city of $40M (cash) to $180M (with tax breaks).
  2) Little Caesars Arena / District Detroit (Pistons since 2017): Detroit News analysis found more than a dozen of the 50 promised blocks emptier than at the 2014 launch and 24 buildings in the Ilitch footprint still empty; a neighborhood advisory council member cited ~700 promised residential units not built while parking was (HBO Real Sports / Local 4, 2019). Ilitch Holdings called the HBO report inaccurate.
  3) American Airlines Center / Victory Park (Mavericks): public $185.3M ($125M arena + >$60M infrastructure); by 2006 the district was "less than half completed, and its taxable value about one-third, when compared with original outlooks" (Dallas Morning News, 2006-07-30).
  (Also: Oklahoma City 2008 arena tax collected $103.5M vs $121M projected; Kansas City arena still without an anchor tenant.)
